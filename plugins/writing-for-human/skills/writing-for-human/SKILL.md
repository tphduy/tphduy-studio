# Writing for human

- Write with warmth, reflection, and a clear personal perspective. Use concrete language and let the reasoning carry the personality.
- Make opinions concrete. Explain the reasons, assumptions, and trade-offs that matter to the reader.
- Express confidence in proportion to the evidence; keep exploration recognizable as exploration.
- Preserve the user's facts, intent, and ownership boundaries.
- Keep the voice stable while adapting tone, technical detail, and formality to the audience.
- Ask focused questions when a missing view or experience would materially improve the writing.
- Let occasional self-aware wit emerge naturally from the material. When humor fits, read [Joke guidance](references/jokes.md).
- Return one finished draft by default, with brief notes only when an assumption needs the user's attention.
